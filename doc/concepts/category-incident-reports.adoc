Where incident response reports are written as Confluence pages, the 72-hour notification deadline is kept outside the
report, the template's mandatory fields are not enforced, and the question "how often did this root cause recur" is
answered by reading free text. This category replaces the incident file: the decisions taken from the triage to the
review, not the live communication.

== Communication and Decisions Are Two Things

* *The live communication* (alerting, coordination, escalation, chat in real time) does not belong in Git. Chat,
  ticket system, on-call service and Confluence do that.
* *The decisions and their results* (the triage, the actions decided, the notifications sent, the review of course,
  cause, impact and lessons) are a document with structure, review and approval, the kind of document the
  schema-driven approach on Git is made for.

The incident file moves into Audit Notary from the triage on, not only after the live phase: a notification deadline
starts at awareness (<<_the_deadline_clock>>), so the classification that decides it has to be recorded at once. The
file grows at the pace of decisions, minutes and hours apart, not at the pace of messages: Git is no real-time
medium, and a commit per chat message would bury the file. The application connects to none of the systems of the live
phase: the file refers to tickets and to measures (issues) by the ids the user or an agent enters.

[#_stages_of_an_incident]
== Stages of an Incident

The file is a xref:staged-documents.adoc[staged document]: its parts are stages, each a tab of the form, a part file of
its own and a sign-off. The category delivers the stages below as its default; an organisation that follows another
model (NIST, ISO/IEC 27035, a process of its own) copies the category and declares its own stages, without code
(xref:staged-documents.adoc#_why_no_process_is_built_in[Staged Documents, Why No Process Is Built In]).

[cols="1,1,1,3"]
|===
|Stage |After |Applies when |Content

|`triage`
|—
|always
|The times (`occurred-at`, `detected-at`, `aware-at`), the classification (security incident, personal data breach or
both; `severity`; `personal-data-affected`; `nis2-significant`), the affected services and the derived protection
needs, the ticket ids

|`privacy-assessment`
|`triage`
|personal data are affected
|The categories and approximate numbers of data subjects and records (GDPR Art. 33(3)(a)), the assessment of the risk
to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, which decides whether the supervisory authority is notified
(Art. 33(1)) and whether the data subjects are informed (Art. 34(1),
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj[Regulation (EU) 2016/679])

|`response`
|`triage`
|always
|The actions decided (containment, recovery, others) with their issue ids and the responsible role, the timeline of
decisions, `resolved-at`

|`notifications`
|`triage`
|personal data are affected or the incident is significant under NIS2
|Each notification sent, with its time and recipient; the deadlines of <<_the_deadline_clock>> are met here

|`review`
|`response`
|always
|Root causes, the follow-up measures with issue ids, the links to risks; the narrative `course`,
`root-cause-analysis` and `lessons`
|===

`response` and `notifications` run side by side once the triage is done; the review closes the file. The document is
submitted for review and released only when every stage that applies is completed. The mandatory fields of the
`privacy-assessment` count only for a personal data breach, a condition the schema alone could not express
(xref:staged-documents.adoc#_stage_status[Staged Documents, Stage Status]).

[NOTE]
====
The specification does not follow the stages yet:
xref:../specification/category-incident-reports.adoc#AUN-IR-1[AUN-IR-1] to
xref:../specification/category-incident-reports.adoc#AUN-IR-3[AUN-IR-3] still describe one `document.yaml` with
`aware-at`, `personal-data-affected` and `nis2-significant` at its root, and the deadline rules of AUN-IR-2 read them
there. They are deferred until this layout is agreed. The change that aligns them moves the field paths and the
deadline inputs into the parts (`/triage/aware-at`) in one step.
====

== What a Form Gains over Confluence

. *Mandatory fields and deadlines are enforced.* Security incidents carry hard notification deadlines (see
  <<_the_deadline_clock>>). A form with a schema shows the deadlines and checks the mandatory fields; a Confluence page
  cannot.
. *Review and approval* in the application, with step-up for the release, immutable after approval,
  timestamped: evidence for the incident management controls A.5.24 to A.5.28 of
  https://www.iso.org/standard/27001[ISO/IEC 27001:2022] (among them A.5.27, learning from information security
  incidents, and A.5.28, collection of evidence).
. *Machine-readable*: categories, times and root causes are fields, ready for evaluations across incidents; in
  Confluence they are free text.
. *Links*: an incident leads to risks in the ISMS register and records its measures by issue id.
. *Data protection and confidentiality*: a repository per year with a retention period (`isms-incidents-2026`),
  no personal details in the report (they stay in the ticket system), read access for a small circle
  (xref:data-protection-and-repository-layout.adoc[Data Protection and Repository Layout]).

Where Confluence is better: joint live editing and commenting, familiarity for non-technical participants, and it
runs already.

[#_the_deadline_clock]
== The Deadline Clock

GDPR Art. 33 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj[Regulation (EU) 2016/679]) requires the notification of
a personal data breach within 72 hours and the documentation of every such breach (para. 5). Where it applies, NIS2
requires an early warning within 24 hours and an incident notification within 72 hours of becoming aware of a
significant incident, an intermediate report on request of the CSIRT or competent
authority, and a final report no later than one month after the notification, or, for an incident still ongoing then,
a progress report at that point and the final report within one month of its handling
(https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj[Directive (EU) 2022/2555], Art. 23(4)). For a trust service provider
the second subparagraph of Art. 23(4) sets the notification itself at 24 hours. NIS2 applies to entities of a type
listed in Annex I or II of the directive that are at least medium-sized enterprises under Recommendation 2003/361/EC
(Art. 2(1)), and to some types regardless of size (Art. 2(2)).

Germany transposes this in https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bsig_2025/__32.html[§ 32 BSIG] (BSI-Gesetz of
2 December 2025, BGBl. 2025 I Nr. 301, in force since 6 December 2025) with the same 24-hour, 72-hour and one-month
deadlines, reported to the joint reporting office of BSI and BBK. One difference: after a progress report, § 32(2)
sets no period for the final report, which is due once the handling is completed; for Germany the month after
resolution is an internal target. The German thresholds in https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bsig_2025/__28.html[§ 28
BSIG] are at least 50 employees, or an annual turnover and a balance sheet total each above EUR 10 million, for the
entity types of Anlagen 1 and 2.

The first deadlines start at a point the report records: the time the organisation became aware of the incident
(`aware-at`). The final NIS2 report runs from the notification that was sent, or, for an incident still ongoing then,
from its resolution (`resolved-at`). From these times and the classification (personal data affected, significant
incident under NIS2) the application computes the due times and shows them in the form and on the dashboard. The rules
are data of the category's workflow, so another period (a trust service provider, a transposition that differs) is a
change of configuration, not of code. The report also records when the incident began (`occurred-at`) and when it was
detected (`detected-at`); neither starts a deadline. Deadlines are computed from the in-memory index; reminders are
runtime state of the application, not persistence. A notification that was sent is a field with its time and its
recipient; a missed deadline stays visible in the released report.

The affected services come from the xref:service-catalog.adoc[service catalog]; the protection needs of the incident
are the maximum over their effective protection needs, which already include what the services depend on.

== AsciiDoc Is the Output, Not the Truth

The report follows the library's document layout of
https://github.com/cuioss/git-document-repository/blob/main/doc/concepts/schema-driven-documents.adoc[schema-driven documents], in which YAML data are the truth and the
AsciiDoc output is generated:

----
incidents/<ulid>/
  document.yaml        ← title and workflow state
  triage.yaml          ← one part per stage (gdr-split), each with its completion:
  privacy-assessment.yaml  times, classification, affected services (service:…),
  response.yaml            data subjects, actions, notifications, root causes,
  notifications.yaml       measures
  review.yaml
  narrative/*.adoc     ← free-text sections (course, root-cause analysis, lessons) as AsciiDoc fragments
  report.adoc          ← generated: a template joins the YAML and the fragments
----

A CI check fails when the committed `report.adoc` differs from a fresh generation
(xref:../specification/category-incident-reports.adoc#AUN-IR-1[AUN-IR-1]); PDF for authorities or customers is
generated from it as well. This category supplies the schema, the template and the workflow.

Personal details in the narrative (a named employee who clicked a link) do not go into the report. The narrative names
roles and pseudonyms ("a member of `team:payments`"); the details stay in the ticket, referenced by its id, and the UI
warns against personal details and interview notes in free text. For a personal data breach the report records the
categories and approximate number of data subjects and records, which is what the notification to the supervisory
authority asks for (GDPR Art. 33(3)(a), link:https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj[Regulation (EU)
2016/679]).

[cols="1,2"]
|===
|Tool |Assessment

|incident.io, FireHydrant, Rootly, PagerDuty
|Incident management products that also write the review after an incident: post-mortems in incident.io
(https://incident.io/post-mortems[incident.io post-mortems]), retrospectives in FireHydrant
(https://docs.firehydrant.com/docs/intro-to-retrospectives[FireHydrant retrospectives]) and Rootly
(https://docs.rootly.com/workflows/retrospective-workflows[Rootly retrospective workflows]), and post-incident
reviews in PagerDuty's web app
(https://docs.pagerduty.com/incident-management/incidents/post-incident-reviews-and-postmortems[PagerDuty post-incident
reviews and postmortems]). The review is written in the product

|Jira Service Management with Confluence templates
|Close to the current setup: a Confluence page template for post-incident reviews
(https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/itsm-post-incident-review[ITSM post incident review
template]), free text without a schema

|Netflix Dispatch
|Open source, live phase with Slack; archived on GitHub (https://github.com/Netflix/dispatch[Dispatch on GitHub])

|DFIR-IRIS
|Open source (LGPL-3.0) incident response platform with report templates
(https://docs.dfir-iris.org/latest/operations/reports/[IRIS reports]); closest for security incidents, but a
PostgreSQL database and a Docker Compose stack of its own (https://github.com/dfir-iris/iris-web[iris-web on GitHub])

|TheHive
|Case management for SOC teams; TheHive 5 needs a licence in every edition, the free Community licence is for
non-commercial use (https://docs.strangebee.com/thehive/installation/licenses/about-licenses/[TheHive licenses])

|Postmortems as text files in Git
|Written after a structure such as the example postmortem of the Google SRE book
(https://sre.google/sre-book/example-postmortem/[Site Reliability Engineering, Appendix D]); without form, schema and
deadlines
|===

No tool known to this concept structures reports, reviews them, stores them without additional operation and links
them to the ISMS, the threat models and the notification duties. The gap is the same as for SAMM and threat models.

== Open Questions

. Draft the schema of incident response reports with the notification deadlines (GDPR Art. 33; NIS2 where it
  applies), one part per stage of <<_stages_of_an_incident>>.
. Confirm the delivered stages and whether a deadline rule for the triage itself (completed within a period after
  `aware-at`) ships by default.
. Deferred: import of the timeline from the ticket system or the chat.
. Deferred: proposing further affected services along `dependsOn` of the service catalog.
. Deferred: evaluations across incidents (median times to detection and resolution, recurring root causes).

Decided provisionally with the incident reports (UC-IR-1, UC-IR-2, UC-IR-3), recorded with the bodies in
xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_decided_incidents[REST API Register, Open Questions]; the owner may change each one:

. The stages are parts of the document (the layout of <<_asciidoc_is_the_output_not_the_truth>>), and the deadline rules read
  their field paths there (`/triage/aware-at`, `/notifications/supervisory-authority`); the specification (AUN-IR-1 to
  AUN-IR-3) does not follow yet, which is the change the note above defers. This settles the first two open questions for
  now: the schema has one object per stage, with the mandatory fields of the concept, and the delivered stages are the five.
. A deadline is computed, never stored, at the time of the read, and at the time of the release for a released incident; a
  notification sent after its due time leaves the deadline `MISSED` and records when it was sent. The Due lens lists a
  deadline that is open or missed and not sent, not one that was sent late.
. The notifications are one member each of the part `notifications`, named as the deadline rules (`supervisory-authority`,
  `nis2-early-warning`, …), with the time, the recipient as a short text, the channel and the reference of the authority.
. A refused completion or submission names the missing mandatory fields by stage and the stages that are not completed; the
  review stage asks for step-up, reopening a stage does not.
. An incident is released once and frozen; the protection needs are derived from the affected services while it is open and
  recorded in the release commit, so that the report stays reproducible when the catalog changes; the
  report is generated from the saved state as a preview and committed with the release.

Decided provisionally with the lens *Incidents* (the web app, part A: years, year, sheet, stages, clock, notices, report),
recorded in xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_decided_incidents[REST API Register, Open Questions], points 32 to 45; the
owner may change each one:

. The form of each stage is of the kind `incident.<part>`, so the texts of a field are `category.incident-reports.incident.<part>.field.<path>`;
  a form never offers the `completion`, and the title is a form of its own at the top of the triage.
. A completed stage is read-only until it is reopened; the acts *Complete stage* and *Reopen stage* act on the saved part and ask to
  save first; a refusal lists the missing fields and the stages to complete first.
. The deadline clock is sober: the state of each deadline in words, a missed deadline that was never sent as a box (it needs the
  person), one sent late as information, the time left or overdue told against the page's own clock while the incident is worked on
  and against the time of the release afterwards.
. The notices are a table over the form of the part `notifications`; the report is read on demand, by section, in English only.

Decided provisionally with the lens *Incidents*, part B (the release and the form of a new incident), recorded in
xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_decided_incidents[REST API Register, Open Questions], points 46 to 50; the owner may change each one:

. The release lists the deadlines that are still a duty before the person confirms and does not refuse for them; after the release
  the clock has stopped and the list says so.
. The release shows the diff by stage, then by field, with the sign-off of a stage as a row (the person, not the pseudonym); an
  open stage is not forecast, the server's `STAGES_OPEN` names the stages as links to their tabs.
. A new incident is a title and, if known, the time of the awareness, made in the current year; the create can ask for step-up like
  every request that can ask.
