Status: *the name is decided: Audit Notary* (owner's decision of 2026-11-22). It replaces *TrailHand*, decided on
2026-11-20, because a reader whose first language is not English cannot tell from that name what the application is
(<<_why_trailhand_was_dropped>>). The rename was made in one sweep on 2026-11-22 (<<_the_rename>>); the SonarCloud
project key followed the same day. The mascot is accepted (<<_the_mascot>>), the mark is still a proposal, and the
trademark search of <<_open_decisions>> is still to be done.

This page records what was checked, why the working title *CISOs-Friend* and then *TrailHand* were dropped, and what
each rename changes.

== Why the Working Title Needed a Decision

* *Close to an established product.* https://github.com/intuitem/ciso-assistant-community[CISO Assistant] is an
  open-source governance, risk and compliance platform by intuitem, with a hosted offering; a 2026 article describes it
  as open-source cybersecurity management and GRC
  (https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/01/14/ciso-assistant-open-source-cybersecurity-management-grc/[Help Net
  Security, 14 January 2026]). "CISOs-Friend" and "CISO Assistant" would be confused in a search and in a
  conversation, in the same field.
* *Narrower than the audience.* The application serves development teams that fill in assessments, incident responders
  and AI agents as well as CISOs (xref:audit-notary-overview.adoc#_audience[Overview, Audience]). A name built on one
  of the roles describes the buyer, not the work.
* *Hard to say and to type.* The possessive and the hyphen give an identifier no one can predict (`cisos-friend`,
  `CISOs-Friend`, `CISO's Friend`).

The strength of the working title is that it says in three words what the application is for.

== The Brand Family

The libraries and tools of the author share one brand language: a cartoon character in an outfit that says the role of
the project. The character started as a guinea pig and is now drawn as a bear; this documentation calls it the
family's bear. The family images are in
https://github.com/cuioss/cuioss-parent-pom/tree/main/doc/images[`cuioss-parent-pom/doc/images`] (a sheriff, a
detective, a quality controller with a tape measure, a craftsman, among others); their licence file
(https://github.com/cuioss/cuioss-parent-pom/blob/main/doc/images/image-license.adoc[`image-license.adoc`]) states that
they were generated with ChatGPT (DALL·E) for the project and are released under Apache License 2.0. The plan-marshall
web app turned that language into a small design system
(https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-clients/blob/main/doc/design/web-app/brand-mark.adoc[`brand-mark.adoc` of
`pm-mcp-clients`]). Its rules are taken over here, because the web app of this application follows `pm-web-app`

[#_how_the_family_names_work]
=== How the Family's Names Work

The names of the neighbouring projects are understood without an explanation, also by readers whose first language is
not English. They share one pattern: the first word names *what the project works on*, the second names *a role*, and
the role is the outfit of the mascot.

[cols="1,1,1,2"]
|===
|Project |What it works on |Role |Mascot

|https://github.com/cuioss/TokenSheriff[Token Sheriff]
|Token
|Sheriff
|A stern bear with a sheriff's star and a revolver

|https://github.com/cuioss/API-Sheriff[API-Sheriff]
|API
|Sheriff
|The family's sheriff

|https://github.com/cuioss/plan-marshall[Plan Marshall]
|Plan
|Marshal
|A bear with a marshal's star on the hat and a clipboard headed `PLAN`
|===

Three properties carry the effect:

* *Both words are basic vocabulary.* Token, API and Plan are words every developer knows; sheriff and marshal are known
  far beyond English from the western genre.
* *The role says what the project does with the object.* A sheriff enforces the rules on tokens; a marshal puts a plan
  in order (_to marshal_ also means to arrange).
* *The name can be drawn.* The outfit and one prop say the role, so the mascot, the mark and the tagline follow from
  the name instead of explaining it.

[#_why_trailhand_was_dropped]
== Why TrailHand Was Dropped

*TrailHand* (decided 2026-11-20) carried a narrative: the _trail_ was the audit trail of signed commits, the _hand_ the
ranch hand who does the legwork. Measured against <<_how_the_family_names_work>>, the narrative does not reach the
reader:

. *"Trail" does not say "audit".* Only the compound _audit trail_ carries the meaning, and it is the vocabulary of
  auditors and native speakers. On its own, _trail_ is a path through terrain; in German it is everyday vocabulary from
  trail running and mountain biking. Learners of English also confuse _trail_ with _trial_, so the name is misheard and
  mistyped.
. *"Hand" is not a role outside English.* _Ranch hand_ and _farmhand_ are idioms; everyone else reads the part of the
  body. The name therefore has no role word, which is the part that makes the family's names work.
. *Neither word names the object.* Token, API and Plan say what the project is about; neither _trail_ nor _hand_ says
  documents, audit or security.
. *The mascot cannot show the name.* A bear in a cowboy hat without a star reads as a cowboy, or as a sheriff without
  the badge. Nothing in the picture says _trail_ or _hand_.
. *The name needed a paragraph.* The decision had to explain both words; a name that needs an explanation has failed
  for the readers it is meant for.
. *It sits close to Trailhead,* the learning platform of another vendor (see <<_spelling>> of the earlier decision);
  the capital _H_ only helps in writing, not in speech.

== Name Candidates

Criteria, sharpened after TrailHand:

. *Two words of basic vocabulary,* understood by a developer or a CISO whose first language is not English, ideally
  words that other European languages have borrowed.
. *The first word names the object.* _Audit_ fits best: it is the CISO's own word, it is the same in German and
  French, and every one of the four categories is evidence for an audit.
. *The second word names a role with one prop,* so that the outfit, the mark and the tagline follow from the name.
. *The role stands for care and record keeping, not enforcement,* as the voice of the application does (<<_voice>>):
  unarmed, and a release stays with a human (xref:mcp-server.adoc[MCP Server]).
. *No collision* with a product in security, compliance or developer tooling.

=== Decision

*Audit Notary.* The object is the audit, the role is the notary: the one who signs, dates and keeps the record, and who
does not decide what it says. The name carries the two promises the design makes: every change is signed and
attributable (xref:../specification/authentication.adoc[Authentication]), and the application does the record keeping
for the person, in the web app or through an agent, while a release stays with a human (xref:mcp-server.adoc[MCP
Server]).

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Line |Text

|Name
|Audit Notary

|Claim
|Every change signed. Every version on record.

|Descriptor, under the logo
|Security documents on the Git server you already run.
|===

The working title's phrase _the CISO's friend_ is no longer the descriptor: it is an idiom as well, and the claim now
says the promise directly.

*The notary is a metaphor, not a legal claim.* A notary has a legal function, and readers in compliance take words
literally. No text of the application, its documentation or its marketing says that a document is _notarised_ or has
the evidential value of a notarial deed. The wording is _signed_, _dated_, _attributable_ and _on record_.

== The Mark

*Idea:* a document page with a folded corner and a *round seal* on its lower half, the notary's seal on the record. The
seal has a scalloped edge of many small rounded lobes, a plain centre and the gold of the palette. It says "a signed
record" without a star, a shield or a padlock, the three symbols every security product carries.

* *Drawing rules* are those of the family (<<_the_brand_family>>): `24 × 24` grid, hand-drawn, solid fills with the dark
  outline, a one-colour rendering with the seal cut out, no text in the drawing.
* *Small sizes.* At `16` px the mark reduces to the seal alone, which stays legible where the page with its folded
  corner does not.
* *Distance from the sheriff badge:* the seal is round with a scalloped edge and has no points and no ball tips, so it is
  not mistaken for the sign of plan-marshall or Token Sheriff beside which it will sit in a user's tool list.
* *Distance from the gear:* few, large lobes turn the seal into a gear, the common symbol for settings; the first trial
  with twelve lobes read that way. The lobes stay small and many (twenty in the provisional drawing).
* *No ribbons:* a seal with ribbon tails reads as an award or a certificate, which is a claim the application does not
  make (<<_decision>>).
* *The earlier sketch is superseded.* `resources/brand/mark-sketch.svg` shows the TrailHand idea (a page with a trail of
  three commit nodes). It stays as a record of that idea and is not an asset to ship.
* *Provisional drawing in the web app.* Until the final mark is delivered and accepted, the web app carries a
  provisional drawing of the seal idea (`web-app/src/lib/brand/BrandMark.svelte`, the seal alone at `16` px and in the
  favicon `web-app/static/brand-mark.svg`); `MARK_IS_PROVISIONAL` in `web-app/src/lib/brand/brand.ts` marks it. It must
  be replaced by the accepted final mark before the first release.

== The Mascot

The character is the family's bear as the *notary*: a dark suit with a white shirt and a brown tie, round spectacles,
calm and smiling. The suit says what a notary looks like to a European reader: a professional of the office who signs
and keeps the record. He is friendly where the sheriff is stern, and he is unarmed: the product stands for care, not
enforcement. The family has a stern sheriff and a smiling detective; the notary stands next to them as the one who keeps
and signs the record.

*The image is a family image.* The owner chose the quality controller of
https://github.com/cuioss/cuioss-parent-pom/tree/main/doc/images[`cuioss-parent-pom/doc/images`]
(`Quality-Control.png`) as the notary on 2026-11-22; no other project uses it as its mascot. Its prop, a tape measure,
belongs to its first role. A later edit may replace it with the notary's seal stamp and register (the prompt is below);
until then the image is used as it is.

The poses of the web app (*welcome* on the first-run page, *at work* in the empty states, *done* after a release) all
show this one image until pose-specific images are made. No pose shows a person's data or a score. The raster images
follow the asset rules of the family: only derived, size-reduced files are shipped, never the source image. The source
is kept in `resources/brand/` as the master of the derivatives; the documentation is not part of the application.

*Provenance:* the family's images were made with an AI image tool. For each image, record the tool, the prompt and the
owner's acceptance in the table below before it ships. The Apache-2.0 release of the existing family images does not
cover new images automatically; the owner states the licence of each new one.

The origin and the licence of the AI-made images are stated once, in

[#_voice]
== Voice

Plain, calm and accountable: the voice of a notary's office. The people who read the application's pages are
preparing an audit or answering an incident; the tone is that of a colleague who keeps good records. No jokes in error
messages, deadline warnings or anywhere in the incident-report category; the character appears only where nothing is
at stake. The words _notarised_ and _certified_ are not used for what the application does (<<_decision>>).

== Usage Rules

* Use the delivered drawings; do not redraw, recolour, rotate or add a shadow.
* The colour mark does not stand on a plate; the one-colour mark takes the surface's text colour.
* The visible name stays beside the mark except where an accessible name carries it.
* The mark and the character never change with state, and never appear in a generated document that leaves the
  application (an exported report carries the organisation's identity, not the product's).
