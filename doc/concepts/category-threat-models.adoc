A threat model written once in a workshop and not maintained falls behind the architecture it describes, and without a
review date nothing shows that it has. Threat models are the second category of Audit Notary, and its
contribution is not the diagram but the life cycle: recipes that guide the filling in, by people or by an AI agent
through the xref:mcp-server.adoc[MCP server], and a review that falls due when the service's protection needs say so.

No existing system known to this concept maintains threat models across many services and evaluates them without
additional operation. That gap is the place of this category.

== Where a Threat Model Lives

Every threat model lives in the central repository `threat-models`, names its service in a field, and is saved and
released like every other document. The application writes into no repository of a development team, so it needs no pull request
through a Git host API and no write right there, and the repository can be restricted to the readers threat models
call for. Keeping a model beside the code would make an outdated model detectable from the diffs of the code; that
detection is deferred (see <<_open_questions>>).

== What the Application Contributes

Not a diagram editor; Threat Dragon has one. The value lies in the life cycle:

. *Recipes* guide the filling in; the result is a structured report in an open format.
. *Format*: OTM or the JSON of Threat Dragon instead of an own schema. With Threat Dragon JSON teams can go on editing
  the diagram in Threat Dragon and the application reads the same file; with OTM that needs a Threat Dragon release
  that imports OTM (see the open questions).
. *Maintenance*: a review due date from the protection needs of the service, review and release with step-up.
. *Evaluation across services*: open threats per service, untreated threats, age of the models.
. *Links*: threat modelling is a stream of the SAMM practice _Threat Assessment_ (business function _Design_;
  https://owaspsamm.org/model/design/threat-assessment/[SAMM, Threat Assessment]), so
  maintained threat models are direct evidence for that practice in a SAMM assessment; accepted or untreated
  threats become entries of the ISMS risk register (`isms-register:risk/…`), countermeasures record their issue ids.

== What to Observe

* *Confidentiality*: threat models describe attack paths; the `threat-models` repository is restricted.
* *Personal data*: hardly any, apart from owners; roles instead of names, as elsewhere.
* *Service*: every threat model refers to its entry in the service catalog (`service:…`)
  (xref:service-catalog.adoc[Service Catalog]).
* *Diagrams* are stored as data (OTM or JSON); images are generated, so diffs stay readable.

[#_open_questions]
== Open Questions

. Decide the threat model format (OTM or Threat Dragon JSON). Teams that edit in Threat Dragon need its JSON, or a
  Threat Dragon release that imports OTM (see <<_existing_tools>>).
. How the model file fits the library's document layout, which knows YAML data and AsciiDoc fragments
  (https://github.com/cuioss/git-document-repository/blob/main/doc/specification/git-forms.adoc#GDR-FORMS-3[GDR-FORMS-3]): a declared split with a JSON codec, or the
  model stored as YAML, which OTM allows and Threat Dragon JSON does not.
. Which recipes ship with the application, and whether a recipe is itself a schema-driven document.
. Deferred: detection of an outdated model from the diffs of the service's repositories since the last review.

Decided provisionally with the Reports lens (UC-TM-4), recorded in
xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_decided_reports[REST API Register, Open Questions]; the owner may change each one:

. The evaluation across services is one request, `GET /api/v1/reports/threat-evaluation`, one row per threat model with its
  service, the highest effective protection need, the review due date and the open, untreated and transferred threats;
  the lens *Threat models* and the report show the same answer.
. An untreated threat is open, has no countermeasure and is not transferred; a model that was never released is
  `unreleased` and has no review date; the services without a model are listed beside the table, a planned one too.

Decided provisionally with the threat models (UC-TM-1, UC-TM-2, UC-TM-4), recorded with the bodies in
xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_decided_threat_models[REST API Register, Open Questions]; the owner may change each one:

. The format is OTM, serialised as YAML in the file `model.yaml` beside `document.yaml` (the schema's `gdr-split`), which settles
  the first two open questions for now. The model is a subset of OTM: project, trust zones, components, data flows, threats and
  mitigations; every other member is kept unchanged.
. The status of a threat, its countermeasures with their issue ids and the reference of its transfer are kept on the threat in
  `attributes`, not on OTM's instance of a threat on a component; a countermeasure belongs to one threat.
. The diagram is generated from the components and the data flows and served as an image in the read; the file holds no
  coordinates.
. Two recipes ship (`quick-start`, four steps; `stride`, six), as data, in English and German; the recipe is read below the
  category, and a person's unfinished recipe is a draft of a model that does not exist yet, kept by service and recipe.
. A review starts with an act that prefills the review date with today; the due date counts from the date of the last release and
  the interval by the highest protection need is served with the model.

Decided provisionally with the screens of the lens (the lens without the recipe wizard), recorded in
xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_decided_threat_models[REST API Register, Open Questions]; the owner may change each one:

. The sheet of a model keeps one document controller for the data of the document and one for the file of the model, so a save
  of both is two signed commits; whether it should be one needs the shared controller to key its changes by part.
. The threat table and the countermeasure editor are the lens's own, so that a countermeasure names its threat and its issue ids
  can be typed; removing a threat removes its countermeasures in the same save.
. The act *Start the review* is offered to everyone who may edit, in every state, and sets the review date and nothing else.
. The first column of the lens is the table of the report, a service without a model is offered *New threat model* in its row,
  and the address of the wizard is a placeholder inside the lens until the wizard is built.
. The labels of the form are keys of the app's own catalogue, because the server's catalogue of the document type is not
  served yet.
. The release page reads the lists of threats and countermeasures change by change, by id.
